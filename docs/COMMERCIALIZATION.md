This repository is currently distributed under the MIT License. The MIT License permits commercial use, modification, distribution, sublicensing, and sale of copies, subject to its notice requirements.

This document explains the project's commercialization posture. It does not replace or narrow `LICENSE`.

## What MIT means for this repository

Code already distributed under MIT may be used commercially by the owner or by third parties under the MIT terms. Forking, cloning, modifying, or building a commercial product from the MIT-licensed public core is therefore permitted by the license.

Changing repository visibility or changing the license for future code does not revoke rights already granted for previously distributed MIT-licensed versions.

## What the owner can still commercialize

Keeping a public MIT core does not prevent Stock Lookup from monetizing complementary products or services, including:

- hosted Stock Lookup services;
- premium dashboards and research workflows;
- APIs and managed analytics;
- curated or separately licensed data/evidence products;
- enterprise integrations, deployment, support, or consulting;
- private portfolio/risk/execution modules;
- proprietary calibration, operating policies, or other private extensions;
- commercial access to infrastructure or services that are not themselves distributed as part of this MIT repository.

Those offerings may have separate terms where legally and contractually appropriate.

## Project moat

The project should not treat source-code secrecy as its only competitive advantage. Durable differentiation can come from:

- evidence quality and provenance;
- point-in-time discipline;
- source qualification and data continuity;
- calibration and validation history;
- operational reliability;
- portfolio/risk governance;
- execution-quality research;
- maintained datasets and evidence corpora;
- product quality and user trust.

The public core should make the engineering principles inspectable without requiring publication of every operational asset or competitive configuration.

## Third-party contributions

External contributions can affect future licensing flexibility. Contributors must have the right to submit their work and must understand the inbound licensing terms described in `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

For substantial source-code contributions, maintainers may require an additional contributor agreement before merge. This preserves clear intellectual-property provenance and reduces future ambiguity around commercial or dual-distribution arrangements.

## Brand and endorsement

The MIT License governs the software copyright license. It does not authorize anyone to falsely represent a fork, product, or service as being operated, certified, endorsed, or supported by the Stock Lookup maintainer.

Forks should identify material changes and avoid creating confusion about their relationship to the upstream project.

## Data rights are separate

The repository's MIT License applies to the software distributed here. It does not automatically grant redistribution rights to third-party market data, provider responses, exchange data, financial documents, licensed datasets, or other external content.

Before publishing any data artifact, confirm the source's redistribution terms independently.

## Policy review

Before introducing a paid product, accepting large third-party code contributions, changing the repository license, or publishing previously private operational modules, the owner should perform a dedicated licensing/IP review. For material commercial decisions, obtain professional legal advice appropriate to the relevant jurisdiction.
